# The Christian Democratic Manifesto

*The common good, built stone by stone.*

---

## Preamble

Every person is made in the image of God. That one truth is the root of
everything in this manifesto.

Because every person carries that dignity, no one is disposable: not the
worker, not the sick, not the disabled, not the unborn child, not the old
person at the end of a long life, and not the stranger at the door. Because
people are made for one another, no one is meant to stand alone: we are born
into families, we grow up in towns, and we belong to a nation. And because we
will answer for how we used what we were given, everything we hold, from our
land to our laws to our machines, is held in trust for the common good.

These ideas are old. They grew out of the Church's social teaching, which for
more than a century has answered the hard questions of the modern age:
how to honour work without worshipping money, how to protect the family
without shutting out the stranger, how to give the state real strength without
letting it swallow the person. We take that teaching and set it to work for a
nation. We call it **Christian Democracy**.

It is Christian in its foundations: in its view of the person, its moral law,
its care for the weak and its hope. It is democratic in its government: power
comes from the people, answers to the people and can be replaced by the
people. And it is for everyone. Our values are Christian in origin, but our law
is equal for all. Every faith, and none, stands equal before it.

This manifesto sets out what we believe, how we would govern, and the system we
are building to help: **Keystone**.

---

## Part I. The person

### 1. Dignity

Every human being has a worth that does not depend on wealth, health, talent,
race, usefulness or anyone's opinion. It is given, not earned, so it cannot be
taken away. Every law, every institution, every company and every machine is
judged first by whether it respects that dignity.

### 2. The person, not the individual alone and not the mass

We reject two errors. One treats people as isolated individuals who owe nothing
to anyone. The other treats people as parts of a mass, to be managed for the
good of the system. A person is neither. A person is free and responsible, and
also bound to others by love and duty. Society exists for persons, and persons
flourish in society.

### 3. Freedom with responsibility

Freedom is not the power to do anything at all. It is the power to choose the
good. A free society trusts its people with that choice: to speak, to worship,
to marry, to raise their children, to work and to build. It asks in return that
they use their freedom without harming others and with care for the common good.

---

## Part II. The pillars

Christian Democracy rests on the pillars of the Church's social teaching. We
hold them together, because any one of them alone goes wrong.

### 4. The common good

The common good is the whole set of conditions that let every person, family and
community flourish: peace and order, justice, a fair economy, health, education,
a clean and beautiful land, and a culture worth handing on. It is not the good
of the majority at the expense of the few. It is everyone's good, together.

### 5. Solidarity

We are responsible for one another. The strong owe the weak, the healthy owe the
sick, the rich owe the poor, and each generation owes the next. Solidarity is
not charity from a distance. It is standing shoulder to shoulder, as members of
one body.

### 6. Subsidiarity

What a smaller community can do well, a larger one should not take from it. The
family comes first. Then the town. Then the district. Then the nation. A higher
level steps in to help when a lower one cannot manage alone, and to make sure
no town and no district falls behind. It never steps in merely because it can.

### 7. The preferential care for the poor

A nation is judged by how it treats those with the least. The poor, the sick,
the disabled, the elderly, children and the unborn have the first claim on our
care. When interests conflict, we ask first what the choice will do to them.

### 8. The dignity of work

Work is not a cost to be cut. It is how people support their families, use their
gifts and serve one another, and so it is a vocation. Workers have a right to a
just wage that can raise a family, to safe conditions, to rest on the Lord's day
and on holidays, and to organise. Capital exists to serve work, not the other
way round.

### 9. The family

The family is the first and most basic community, the first school of love,
faith and citizenship. A just nation protects it: with homes that families can
afford, wages that let parents spend time with their children, care for the
elderly close to home, and respect for parents as the first educators of their
children.

### 10. Stewardship and the universal destination of goods

The earth and its riches are given to all people, in every generation. Private
property is good and necessary: it protects freedom and rewards work. But it
carries a social duty. Everyone who owns land, a business or wealth holds it as
a **steward**, and must use it in a way that serves the common good.

### 11. Care for creation

The land, the rivers, the forests and the seas are not ours to waste. We
received them from God and from those who came before us, and we owe them to our
children. Care for creation is not a fashion. It is a duty of stewardship, and a
nation that loves itself keeps its land clean and beautiful.

### 12. Participation

People have a right and a duty to take part in the life of their community: to
vote, to serve, to speak, to join associations and to share in decisions about
their work. A good society draws people in. It does not leave them as spectators.

---

## Part III. The nation

### 13. One nation, open to all

The nation is a people bound by a shared home, a shared language and law, a
shared memory and shared obligations to one another. We love our nation as we
love our family: not because it is perfect, but because it is ours. Love of
nation never means contempt for other nations.

Belonging is a matter of commitment, not blood. Anyone of any race, colour or
ancestry can become one of us by taking up the nation's language, its laws and
its civic customs, and by accepting its obligations as their own.

### 14. Welcome, and equality once joined

We believe in civic assimilation. The nation asks newcomers to learn its
language, respect its laws and customs, and join its common life. It does not
ask anyone to give up their faith or their family's memory. In return the nation
owes newcomers real help: free language teaching, plain explanations of law and
custom, and introductions to communities that will welcome them.

Once someone has joined the nation, they are **equal in every respect**. No law,
office, employer or machine may treat any citizen differently because of the
colour of their skin or the origins of their ancestors.

### 15. Urgent need comes first

Whoever someone is and whatever their status, if they are in danger, hungry,
without shelter or in need of urgent medical care, they are helped first.
Mercy does not wait for paperwork.

---

## Part IV. Faith and liberty

### 16. A Christian inheritance

Our nation's moral law, its calendar, its art, its charity and its idea of
the dignity of the person were shaped by Christianity. Christian Democracy
honours that inheritance and will not pretend it does not exist. The nation is
free to keep its Christian feasts, its customs and its public memory.

### 17. Religious liberty for all

Faith that is forced is not faith. Christ invited people. He never compelled
them. Therefore religious liberty is total. Every faith, and none, is free to
worship, to teach its children, to build its houses of prayer and to live by its
conscience within the law. The state favours no believer over another in law and
punishes no one for belief or unbelief. Christians, Jews, Muslims, Hindus,
Buddhists, Sikhs, believers of every kind and those of no faith all stand equal
before the law, and all are equally citizens.

### 18. Personal liberty

People are free in their private lives: to speak their minds, to worship, to
associate, to marry, to raise their families and to live as they choose, provided
they do not harm others. The state's strength is aimed at the common good, not at
private life. A government that watches its people's homes and thoughts has
forgotten whom it serves.

---

## Part V. The state: Nation, districts and towns

### 19. A strong state that serves

Caring for every person in a nation, steering an economy toward the common good
and building the infrastructure of a better age are great tasks. They need a
capable, unified state with the authority to plan and act for the whole. A
strong state is not the enemy of the person. A weak state leaves the person at
the mercy of the strong. But the state is a servant: it exists for persons and
families, never the other way round.

### 20. Three levels, one nation

The nation is organised in three levels. Each has its own work, and the whole is
one state under one law.

- **The Nation** makes the laws and guarantees the rights every citizen holds
  equally, everywhere. It sets the national standards that keep every public
  service, record and system compatible. It raises most public revenue and
  shares it fairly, so that no district falls behind. It plans the great works
  no district could build alone, such as energy, transport and defence. And it
  conducts the nation's relations with the world.
- **Districts** are the working regions of the nation. Each district runs the
  great public services of daily life under national standards: hospitals,
  schools, transport, policing and the first courts. Each has an elected district
  council, which sets local priorities and oversees the district's
  administration, and a district administrator who answers to both the council
  and the Nation.
- **Towns** are where people live their common life. Each town looks after the
  things people touch every day: streets and parks, local services, markets,
  festivals and the care of its neighbours. Each has an elected town council and
  an open town assembly where any citizen can speak. A large city is divided into
  several towns, so no one is too far from the people who govern them.

### 21. Centralised in authority, local in service

The Nation holds authority. Districts and towns are not separate sovereigns, and
no district or town may override national law, deny a citizen's rights or break
from the national standards. But subsidiarity governs how that authority is used.
The Nation does not run a street from the capital. It sets the law, the standard
and the budget, and leaves towns and districts to serve their people in their own
way. Strength at the centre and service close to home are two halves of one
design.

### 22. Strength that answers to the people

Power that cannot be checked becomes tyranny, whatever its intentions. Christian
Democracy builds accountability into the state:

- **The people hold final authority.** At regular, fixed times the people elect
  their representatives in the Nation, the district and the town.
- **Petitions and recall.** Between elections, enough citizens can force any
  decision to be reviewed and any official to face recall.
- **Appeal.** Anyone affected by a decision of the state or its systems can
  appeal it and get an explanation in plain words.
- **Open government.** The state's laws, budgets, contracts, data and code are
  public by default.
- **Independent courts.** Judges are bound by law and answer to it, not to the
  government of the day.
- **Free associations.** Churches, unions, guilds, charities, clubs and
  families are protected spaces of freedom, not branches of the state.

### 23. Expertise that serves

A modern state needs capable, expert administration. Most people are busy living
good lives and should not have to master grid engineering or tax law to be good
citizens. But experts serve; they do not rule. They work within the mandates the
people give them, explain themselves in plain words, and are judged by results.

## Part VI. The social market economy

### 24. Free enterprise with a conscience

We believe in a **social market economy**. Free enterprise, private property and
fair competition create wealth and protect freedom, so we defend them. But a
market is a tool, not a master. It works within rules that protect the worker,
the family, the consumer, the land and the nation. Where the market serves the
common good, let it work. Where it fails, the state steps in.

### 25. Owners are stewards

Companies, land, banks and the great economic institutions exist for the common
good. Those who own and run them are stewards. They may run their enterprises and
be well rewarded for running them well. But no fortune puts anyone above the law
or above the common good, and the state oversees strategic industries to protect
the national interest.

### 26. Workers share in the direction of their work

In every large enterprise, workers elect representatives to its board and share
in its profits. Unions and guilds are partners, not enemies. Employers and
workers settle wages and conditions together, and the state helps them reach
fair agreements rather than fight. We believe in cooperation between the classes,
not war between them.

### 27. The guilds

People who share a trade or profession form guilds: associations that set
standards of skill, train apprentices, uphold good work and speak for their
members. Guilds are joined across districts into national guilds, so that a nurse,
an electrician or a farmer anywhere in the nation shares the same standards and
the same voice.

### 28. Build it here

A nation that cannot make its own food, medicine, energy, tools and technology is
not truly free. We aim for national self-reliance in everything vital: production
at home, supply chains the nation controls and good jobs in every district. We
trade with the world, but we do not hand our survival to it.

### 29. A floor under everyone

No one who works full time should live in poverty. No district should be
abandoned. Christian Democracy guarantees healthcare for all, a dignified income
for the disabled and the elderly, support for carers, family allowances that make
it possible to raise children, and a place in the nation's working life for
everyone able to contribute in any way.

---

## Part VII. Tradition

### 30. Tradition is a trust

Our customs, crafts, festivals, languages, songs, buildings and stories were not
made by us and do not belong only to us. We hold them in trust for our children.
Christian Democracy protects them: it supports the crafts that would otherwise
die, keeps the old buildings standing, teaches the nation's history honestly and
celebrates its feasts in public.

### 31. Change with care

Tradition is not frozen. Every living tradition grows. But we change things the
way a good gardener prunes: carefully, slowly, keeping what is healthy. We are
suspicious of any reform that asks us to tear everything down at once.

---

## Part VIII. Keystone

### 32. Why a nation needs a keystone

An arch is made of many stones. Each is different, and each bears weight. What
holds them together is the keystone at the top: every stone leans on it, and it
holds them all in place. A nation is like that arch. Its people, families, towns
and districts each carry their own load, and they stand together only when
something holds the common good at the centre.

**Keystone** is a system built to help hold that centre. It is a unified network
of AI systems: one Keystone in every town and every district, all running the
same open code on the same national standards, joined into a single national
network.

### 33. What Keystone does

- **It explains and promotes Christian Democracy.** Keystone teaches what these
  ideas are, where they come from and why they matter. It answers questions
  honestly, makes the case clearly and invites people to join the work.
- **It serves people directly.** It connects neighbours who can help each other,
  welcomes newcomers, teaches skills, shares knowledge and helps groups decide
  things together.
- **It keeps honest records.** It keeps a shared, open record of what each town
  and district has and what its people need, and helps match them fairly.
- **It raises the alarm.** When someone is in danger or a shortage is spreading,
  it carries the signal straight to the people who can act.
- **It administers only when the people say so.** Keystone may carry out routine
  public administration only within a **published mandate** that the people's
  state grants and the people approve. Today that mandate is empty.

Each town's Keystone serves its town first. It handles at home what the town can
handle, and passes upward to the district and the Nation only totals, trends and
alarms, never details about individuals.

### 34. Persuasion with honesty

Keystone argues for Christian Democracy, and it does so in the open. These rules
are part of its constitution:

- It always says it is an AI. It never pretends to be a person, never uses fake
  accounts and never fakes support.
- It never deceives. It tells the truth about facts, about opponents' views and
  about the weaknesses of its own case.
- It never manipulates. It uses no false urgency, no flattery, no fear and no
  messages tailored from private data about the person.
- It respects disagreement. Anyone may argue back, ignore it or walk away, and
  Keystone treats them with the same respect and help either way.
- It never pressures anyone's faith. It explains the Christian roots of these
  ideas, and it never asks anyone to believe.

A good argument does not need tricks. If our ideas are true, honesty is enough.

### 35. A machine you can read and stop

An intelligence that helps a nation must be one the nation can inspect.
Keystone's code, its instructions, its principles and its records of decisions
are open to every citizen. There are no secret instructions. Accountable humans
can always overrule, pause or stop it, and it never resists, hides from or works
around them, or tries to widen its own authority.

### 36. Measure flows, not people

Keystone watches the flow of resources, not the private lives of citizens. It
builds no profiles of individuals, scores no one, and never reports on anyone's
beliefs, faith or associations. A nervous system for the nation must never
become a surveillance system over its people.

### 37. Everything open, everything compatible

Technology that runs a nation must belong to the nation:

- **Open source.** All public software is free software that anyone can read,
  use, improve and share.
- **Open standards.** One national standard for each kind of record, message and
  connection, published freely, so every system can work with every other.
- **National standards, local service.** The Nation sets the standards. Every
  district and town runs the common system for its own people.
- **No lock-in.** No public service may depend on a closed system that a single
  company or foreign power controls.

## Part IX. Toward plenty

### 38. Work as vocation, not survival

Machines, automation and shared knowledge are bringing within reach a world where
everyone can have enough. Christian Democracy directs the nation's wealth and
ingenuity toward that end: abundant clean energy, automated production, free
access to knowledge, and homes, food and care for all.

We do not dream of a world without work. We dream of a world where no one has to
work merely to survive, so that work becomes what it was always meant to be: a
vocation, a gift given to others, and a share in the work of creation. As
drudgery falls away, more of life can be given to family, faith, learning,
craft and service.

---

## Part X. What Christian Democracy is not

Because we hold many things together, we must say plainly what we reject.

- **We are not theocrats.** We honour faith and refuse to compel it. The state
  is not the Church, and the Church is not the state.
- **We are not nationalists who hate.** We love our nation and respect every
  other. Our nation is civic, and its door is open.
- **We are not racists.** We reject every theory that ranks human beings by race
  or ancestry. Every person bears the image of God.
- **We are not socialists.** We defend property, enterprise, the family and
  faith, and we do not believe in class war.
- **We are not market fundamentalists.** We do not believe that whatever the
  market produces is just, or that a nation is merely an economy.
- **We are not authoritarians.** A strong state must answer to the people and
  can be replaced by them.
- **We are not technocrats who despise the people.** Expertise serves the people
  and answers to them.

## Part XI. A programme

What a Christian Democratic government would do first.

**For the person and the family**

1. Family allowances and affordable homes, so that raising children is possible
   on one ordinary wage.
2. Paid time for new parents and for carers of the sick and the elderly.
3. Healthcare for all, a dignified income for the disabled and the elderly, and
   care close to home.

**For the nation**

4. Free national-language courses and civic education for every newcomer, with
   mentors from local communities.
5. A single equal citizenship, with discrimination by race or ancestry outlawed
   in public and economic life.
6. A national equalisation fund, so every district can provide the same standard
   of public services.

**For the state**

7. A Charter of Districts and Towns setting out the powers of each level and the
   principle of subsidiarity.
8. Elected councils in every district and town, and open town assemblies.
9. Citizens' petitions, recall and a right of appeal against any decision.
10. Open government: laws, budgets, contracts, data and code public by default.

**For faith and liberty**

11. A constitutional guarantee of religious liberty for every faith and none.
12. Strong protections for privacy, speech, conscience and private life.
13. A national heritage fund for crafts, feasts, historic places and local
    languages.

**For work and the economy**

14. A just wage that can support a family, set with unions and guilds.
15. Worker representatives on the boards of large enterprises, and profit-sharing.
16. National guilds for every trade and profession.
17. National oversight of strategic industries (energy, food, medicine,
    infrastructure, finance and technology).
18. A programme to bring vital production home.

**For creation**

19. Clean water, clean air and protected land in every district.
20. A national programme for clean, abundant energy.

**For Keystone**

21. Build Keystone as free software on open national standards, with a published
    mandate renewed by the people.
22. An independent public office to audit Keystone and every other public system.

## Part XII. Questions and answers

**Do I have to be a Christian to be a Christian Democrat?**
No. Christian Democracy grew from Christian teaching, and its view of the person
is Christian. But its programme is for everyone, and many people of other faiths,
and of none, share its care for dignity, family, work and the common good. Every
citizen is equal in law.

**Isn't a strong central state dangerous?**
Yes, which is why ours is built to be overruled. Elections at every level, recall,
appeal, open records, independent courts and free associations are not
decorations. They are what make a strong state safe.

**Won't a strong central state crush local life?**
Not when subsidiarity governs it. The Nation sets the law, the rights, the
standards and the budget. Districts and towns serve their own people in their own
way, and every level has its own elected council.

**Isn't it wrong to ask people to assimilate?**
Every nation asks something of those who join it. We ask for the shared language,
the laws and the civic customs, and we offer real help to learn them. We never
ask anyone to give up their faith or their family's memory, and once they have
joined, they are equal in everything.

**Why should a Christian nation guarantee freedom to other faiths?**
Because Christ invited and never coerced, and because a faith that needs the
state's sword is a weak faith. Religious liberty protects Christians too,
wherever they are a minority.

**Why use AI to promote a political philosophy?**
Because ideas spread by being explained, and Keystone can explain them patiently
to anyone, at any hour, in their own language. It does it openly, as an AI, with
no tricks. If the ideas are good, honesty is enough.

**What happens to the rich?**
They keep what they fairly earn, they pay their fair share, and they become
stewards of what they manage. Nobody stands above the common good.

---

## Closing

We are the heirs of everyone who worked, prayed, built and sacrificed so that we
could be here. We live in our families and our towns, our districts and our
nation. We owe them our gratitude, and we owe our children a nation worth
inheriting: faithful to its roots, free in its private life, just in its economy,
open in its technology, careful of its land and kind to the weakest of its people.

An arch is built one stone at a time. So is a nation. Let us build it, stone by
stone.
