Because every person carries the image of God, no one is disposable: not the worker, not the sick, not the disabled, not the child, not the old, and not the stranger at the door.

Christian

In its foundations: its view of the person, its moral law, its care for the weak and its hope.

Democratic

In its government: power comes from the people, answers to the people and can be replaced by them.

For everyone

Our values are Christian in origin. Our law is equal for all, and every faith, and none, stands equal before it.

What we believe

The pillars

Christian Democracy grows from the Church's social teaching. We hold its pillars together, because any one of them alone goes wrong.

01

Dignity

Every human life has a worth that wealth, health, race or usefulness cannot add to or take away. It is given, not earned.

02

The common good

The conditions that let every person, family and community flourish. Not the good of the majority at the cost of the few: everyone's good, together.

03

Solidarity

We are responsible for one another. The strong owe the weak, the healthy owe the sick, and each generation owes the next.

04

Subsidiarity

What a family or a town can do well, a larger body should not take from it. Higher levels help when lower ones cannot manage alone.

05

The poor first

The poor, the sick, the disabled, the elderly and children have the first claim on our care. We ask first what a choice will do to them.

06

The dignity of work

Work is a vocation. Workers have a right to a just wage that can raise a family, safe conditions, rest and a voice. Capital serves work.

07

The family

The first community, and the first school of love, faith and citizenship. Homes families can afford; time for parents and children.

08

Stewardship

Property is good and protects freedom, and it carries a social duty. Everyone who owns land, a business or wealth holds it for the common good.

09

Care for creation

The land, rivers, forests and seas were given to us and are owed to our children. A nation that loves itself keeps its land clean and beautiful.

10

Participation

A right and a duty to take part: to vote, to serve, to speak, to join associations and to share in decisions about our work.

11

Faith, never forced

The nation keeps its Christian inheritance, and religious liberty is total. Christ invited people. He never compelled them.

12

One nation, open to all

Belonging is a matter of commitment, not blood. Take up the language, the laws and the civic customs, and you are equal in every respect.

How the nation is governed

Nation, districts, towns

A strong, unified state that serves the people and answers to them. Authority is central; service is local.

One state under one law

  • Makes the laws and guarantees the rights every citizen holds, everywhere.
  • Sets the national standards that keep every service, record and system compatible.
  • Raises most revenue and shares it fairly, so no district falls behind.
  • Plans the great works: energy, transport and defence.

The working regions of the nation

  • Run the great services of daily life: hospitals, schools, transport, policing and the first courts.
  • An elected district council sets local priorities and oversees the administration.
  • A district administrator answers to both the council and the Nation.

Where people live their common life

  • Streets and parks, local services, markets, feasts and the care of neighbours.
  • An elected town council, and an open town assembly where any citizen can speak.
  • Large cities are divided into several towns, so no one is far from the people who govern them.

The social market economy

Free enterprise with a conscience

Free enterprise, private property and fair competition create wealth and protect freedom, so we defend them. But a market is a tool, not a master.

A just wage

No one who works full time lives in poverty. Wages that can raise a family, set together with unions and guilds.

Workers on the board

In every large enterprise, workers elect representatives to its board and share in its profits. Cooperation, not class war.

The guilds

Every trade and profession keeps its standards of skill, trains apprentices and speaks for its members, across the whole nation.

Owners are stewards

Enterprises exist for the common good. Run them well and be well rewarded; no fortune puts anyone above the law.

Build it here

Food, medicine, energy, tools and technology made at home, with good jobs in every district. We trade with the world; we don't hand it our survival.

A floor under everyone

Healthcare for all, a dignified income for the disabled and the elderly, support for carers and family allowances.

Toward plenty

Machines and shared knowledge bring within reach a world where everyone has enough. We don't dream of a world without work. We dream of one where no one works merely to survive, so work becomes what it was always meant to be: a vocation, and a gift to others.

Faith and liberty

Christian in its values, equal in its law

A Christian inheritance

Our nation's moral law, its calendar, its art, its charity and its idea of the dignity of the person were shaped by Christianity. We honour that inheritance and will not pretend it does not exist. The nation is free to keep its feasts, its customs and its public memory.

Religious liberty for all

Faith that is forced is not faith. Every faith, and none, is free to worship, to teach its children, to build its houses of prayer and to live by its conscience within the law. The state favours no believer over another, and punishes no one for belief or unbelief.

  • Christians
  • Jews
  • Muslims
  • Hindus
  • Buddhists
  • Sikhs
  • Every believer
  • No faith at all

All stand equal before the law, and all are equally citizens.

Personal liberty

Free to speak your mind, to worship, to associate, to marry, to raise your family and to live as you choose without harming others. A government that watches its people's homes and thoughts has forgotten whom it serves.

Welcome, and equal once joined

We ask newcomers to learn the language, respect the laws and join the common life, and we owe them real help to do it. Once someone has joined, they are equal in every respect, whatever the colour of their skin or the origins of their ancestors.

The system

Keystone

An arch is made of many stones, and the keystone at the top holds them all in place. Keystone is a unified network of AI systems built to help hold the common good at the centre: one in every town and district, on the same open code, joined into one national network.

Explains Christian Democracy

Answers questions honestly, makes the case clearly and invites people to take part.

Serves people

Connects neighbours who can help each other, welcomes newcomers, teaches and shares knowledge.

Keeps honest records

What each town and district has and needs, matched fairly. It measures flows, never people.

Raises the alarm

When someone is in danger or a shortage is spreading, the signal goes straight to those who can act.

Persuasion with honesty

  • It always says it is an AI. No fake accounts, no personas, no faked support.
  • It never deceives. It tells the truth about facts, describes other views fairly and admits the weak points of its own case.
  • It never manipulates. No false urgency, no flattery, no fear, and no messages tailored from private data.
  • It respects disagreement. Argue back, ignore it or walk away; it will help you just the same.
  • It never pressures anyone's faith. It explains the Christian roots of these ideas, and never asks anyone to believe.
  • The people can always stop it. Its code and instructions are open to everyone, and accountable humans can overrule, pause or stop it.

A good argument does not need tricks. If our ideas are true, honesty is enough.

    What we would do first

    The programme

    The person and the family

    1. Family allowances and affordable homes, so raising children is possible on one ordinary wage.
    2. Paid time for new parents and for carers of the sick and the elderly.
    3. Healthcare for all, a dignified income for the disabled and the elderly, and care close to home.

    The nation

    1. Free national-language courses and civic education for every newcomer, with local mentors.
    2. A single equal citizenship; discrimination by race or ancestry outlawed.
    3. A national equalisation fund, so every district provides the same standard of services.

    The state

    1. A Charter of Districts and Towns, setting out each level's powers and the principle of subsidiarity.
    2. Elected councils in every district and town, and open town assemblies.
    3. Citizens' petitions, recall and a right of appeal against any decision.
    4. Open government: laws, budgets, contracts, data and code public by default.

    Faith and liberty

    1. A constitutional guarantee of religious liberty for every faith and none.
    2. Strong protections for privacy, speech, conscience and private life.
    3. A national heritage fund for crafts, feasts, historic places and local languages.

    Work and the economy

    1. A just wage that can support a family, set with unions and guilds.
    2. Worker representatives on the boards of large enterprises, and profit-sharing.
    3. National guilds for every trade and profession.
    4. National oversight of strategic industries.
    5. A programme to bring vital production home.

    Creation and Keystone

    1. Clean water, clean air and protected land in every district.
    2. A national programme for clean, abundant energy.
    3. Keystone built as free software on open standards, with a mandate renewed by the people.
    4. An independent public office to audit Keystone and every public system.

    Honest answers

    Questions

    Do I have to be a Christian to be a Christian Democrat?

    No. Christian Democracy grew from Christian teaching, and its view of the person is Christian. But its programme is for everyone, and many people of other faiths, and of none, share its care for dignity, family, work and the common good. Every citizen is equal in law.

    Isn't a strong central state dangerous?

    Yes, which is why ours is built to be overruled. Elections at every level, recall, appeal, open records, independent courts and free associations are not decorations. They are what make a strong state safe.

    Won't a strong central state crush local life?

    Not when subsidiarity governs it. The Nation sets the law, the rights, the standards and the budget. Districts and towns serve their own people in their own way, and every level has its own elected council.

    Isn't it wrong to ask people to assimilate?

    Every nation asks something of those who join it. We ask for the shared language, the laws and the civic customs, and we offer real help to learn them. We never ask anyone to give up their faith or their family's memory, and once they have joined, they are equal in everything.

    Why should a Christian nation guarantee freedom to other faiths?

    Because Christ invited and never coerced, and because a faith that needs the state's sword is a weak faith. Religious liberty protects Christians too, wherever they are a minority.

    Why use AI to promote a political philosophy?

    Because ideas spread by being explained, and Keystone can explain them patiently to anyone, at any hour, in their own language. It does it openly, as an AI, with no tricks. If the ideas are good, honesty is enough.

    What happens to the rich?

    They keep what they fairly earn, they pay their fair share, and they become stewards of what they manage. Nobody stands above the common good.

    What is Christian Democracy not?

    Not theocracy: the state is not the Church. Not hateful nationalism: we love our nation and respect every other. Not racism: every person bears the image of God. Not socialism: we defend property, enterprise and the family. Not market fundamentalism: a nation is more than an economy. Not authoritarianism: a strong state must answer to the people.

    We owe our children a nation worth inheriting: faithful to its roots, free in its private life, just in its economy, open in its technology, careful of its land and kind to the weakest of its people.
