The Christian Unionist Manifesto

The common good, built stone by stone.

Preamble

Every person is made in the image of God. That one truth is the root of everything in this manifesto.

Because every person carries that dignity, no one is disposable: not the worker, not the sick, not the disabled, not the unborn child, not the old person at the end of a long life, and not the stranger at the door. Because people are made for one another, no one is meant to stand alone: we are born into families, we grow up in towns, and we belong to a nation. And because we will answer for how we used what we were given, everything we hold, from our land to our laws to our machines, is held in trust for the common good.

These ideas are old. They grew out of the Church's social teaching, which for more than a century has answered the hard questions of the modern age: how to honour work without worshipping money, how to protect the family without shutting out the stranger, how to give the state real strength without letting it swallow the person. We take that teaching and set it to work for a nation. We call it Christian Unionism: union with God, with one another and with the generations before and after us, and a union of people of every background in one common good. It is the philosophy of the Christian Union Party.

It is Christian in its foundations: in its view of the person, its moral law, its care for the weak and its hope. It is democratic in its government: power comes from the people, answers to the people and can be replaced by the people. And it is for everyone. Our values are Christian in origin, but our law is equal for all. Every faith, and none, stands equal before it.

The Christian Union Party is an American political party. We work inside the Constitution of the United States and the constitutions of the states: we seek office in free elections, we respect Congress, the courts and the separation of powers, and we change laws only through the people's representatives. We are founded in Arizona, and our first goal is to become a recognized political party there, so that Arizonans can vote for Christian Unionist candidates. Kits to start the party in other states will follow.

This manifesto sets out what we believe, how we would govern, and the system we would build to help: Keystone.

Part I. The person

1. Dignity

Every human being has a worth that does not depend on wealth, health, talent, race, usefulness or anyone's opinion. It is given, not earned, so it cannot be taken away. Every law, every institution, every company and every machine is judged first by whether it respects that dignity.

2. The person, not the individual alone and not the mass

We reject two errors. One treats people as isolated individuals who owe nothing to anyone. The other treats people as parts of a mass, to be managed for the good of the system. A person is neither. A person is free and responsible, and also bound to others by love and duty. Society exists for persons, and persons flourish in society.

3. Freedom with responsibility

Freedom is not the power to do anything at all. It is the power to choose the good. A free society trusts its people with that choice: to speak, to worship, to marry, to raise their children, to work and to build. It asks in return that they use their freedom without harming others and with care for the common good.

Part II. The pillars

Christian Unionism rests on the pillars of the Church's social teaching. We hold them together, because any one of them alone goes wrong.

4. The common good

The common good is the whole set of conditions that let every person, family and community flourish: peace and order, justice, a fair economy, health, education, a clean and beautiful land, and a culture worth handing on. It is not the good of the majority at the expense of the few. It is everyone's good, together.

5. Solidarity

We are responsible for one another. The strong owe the weak, the healthy owe the sick, the rich owe the poor, and each generation owes the next. Solidarity is not charity from a distance. It is standing shoulder to shoulder, as members of one body.

6. Subsidiarity

What a smaller community can do well, a larger one should not take from it. The family comes first. Then the city or town. Then the county. Then the state. Then the nation. A higher level steps in to help when a lower one cannot manage alone, and to make sure no town and no county falls behind. It never steps in merely because it can.

7. The preferential care for the poor

A nation is judged by how it treats those with the least. The poor, the sick, the disabled, the elderly, children and the unborn have the first claim on our care. When interests conflict, we ask first what the choice will do to them.

8. The dignity of work

Work is not a cost to be cut. It is how people support their families, use their gifts and serve one another, and so it is a vocation. Workers have a right to a just wage that can raise a family, to safe conditions, to rest on the Lord's day and on holidays, and to organise. Capital exists to serve work, not the other way round.

9. The family

The family is the first and most basic community, the first school of love, faith and citizenship. A just nation protects it: with homes that families can afford, wages that let parents spend time with their children, care for the elderly close to home, and respect for parents as the first educators of their children.

10. Stewardship and the universal destination of goods

The earth and its riches are given to all people, in every generation. Private property is good and necessary: it protects freedom and rewards work. But it carries a social duty. Everyone who owns land, a business or wealth holds it as a steward, and must use it in a way that serves the common good.

11. Care for creation

The land, the rivers, the forests and the seas are not ours to waste. We received them from God and from those who came before us, and we owe them to our children. Care for creation is not a fashion. It is a duty of stewardship, and a nation that loves itself keeps its land clean and beautiful.

12. Participation

People have a right and a duty to take part in the life of their community: to vote, to serve, to speak, to join associations and to share in decisions about their work. A good society draws people in. It does not leave them as spectators.

Part III. The nation

13. One nation, open to all

The nation is a people bound by a shared home, a shared language and law, a shared memory and shared obligations to one another. We love our nation as we love our family: not because it is perfect, but because it is ours. Love of nation never means contempt for other nations.

Belonging is a matter of commitment, not blood. Anyone of any race, colour or ancestry can become one of us by taking up the nation's language, its laws and its civic customs, and by accepting its obligations as their own.

14. Welcome, and equality once joined

We believe in civic assimilation. The nation asks newcomers to learn its language, respect its laws and customs, and join its common life. It does not ask anyone to give up their faith or their family's memory. In return the nation owes newcomers real help: free language teaching, plain explanations of law and custom, and introductions to communities that will welcome them.

Once someone has joined the nation, they are equal in every respect. No law, office, employer or machine may treat any citizen differently because of the colour of their skin or the origins of their ancestors.

15. Urgent need comes first

Whoever someone is and whatever their status, if they are in danger, hungry, without shelter or in need of urgent medical care, they are helped first. Mercy does not wait for paperwork.

Part IV. Faith and liberty

16. A Christian inheritance

Our nation's moral law, its calendar, its art, its charity and its idea of the dignity of the person were shaped by Christianity. Christian Unionism honours that inheritance and will not pretend it does not exist. The nation is free to keep its Christian feasts, its customs and its public memory.

17. Religious liberty for all

Faith that is forced is not faith. Christ invited people. He never compelled them. Therefore religious liberty is total. Every faith, and none, is free to worship, to teach its children, to build its houses of prayer and to live by its conscience within the law. The state favours no believer over another in law and punishes no one for belief or unbelief. Christians, Jews, Muslims, Hindus, Buddhists, Sikhs, believers of every kind and those of no faith all stand equal before the law, and all are equally citizens.

18. Personal liberty

People are free in their private lives: to speak their minds, to worship, to associate, to marry, to raise their families and to live as they choose, provided they do not harm others. The state's strength is aimed at the common good, not at private life. A government that watches its people's homes and thoughts has forgotten whom it serves.

Part V. Government: within the American constitutional order

19. A capable government that serves

Caring for every person, steering an economy toward the common good and building the infrastructure of a better age are great tasks. They need a capable government with the authority and the tools to act. A strong government is not the enemy of the person. A weak one leaves the person at the mercy of the strong. But government is a servant: it exists for persons and families, never the other way round.

20. The levels we work within

America already has the layered government that subsidiarity asks for, and we keep it.

  • The federal government. Congress makes federal law, the President executes it, and the courts judge under the Constitution. We seek seats in Congress and support a federal government that keeps to its constitutional role and does it well.
  • The states. Each state governs most of daily life: schools, roads, courts, health programs, water, licensing and elections. This is where Christian Unionism can do the most good first, and Arizona is where we begin.
  • Counties. Counties run courts, elections, public health and services across their whole area, under state law.
  • Cities and towns. They look after what people touch every day: streets, parks, police and fire, water and local services.

21. One connected state, local service

Within a state, government should work as one. Today a family that needs help from three state agencies tells the same story three times, on three websites, and waits three times. The Christian Union Party would connect the state's own systems: one secure account, shared records that the citizen controls, common open standards, and one assistant, Keystone, that can help a person through all of it. The state sets the standards and builds the shared systems; counties, cities and towns keep their own powers under the state constitution and can join the shared systems so their residents get the same quality of service. Strength at the centre and service close to home are two halves of one design.

22. Strength that answers to the people

Power that cannot be checked becomes tyranny, whatever its intentions. Christian Democracy builds accountability into the state:

  • The people hold final authority. At regular, fixed times the people elect their representatives at every level of government.
  • Petitions and recall. Between elections, enough citizens can force any decision to be reviewed and any official to face recall.
  • Appeal. Anyone affected by a decision of the state or its systems can appeal it and get an explanation in plain words.
  • Open government. The state's laws, budgets, contracts, data and code are public by default.
  • Independent courts. Judges are bound by law and answer to it, not to the government of the day.
  • Free associations. Churches, unions, guilds, charities, clubs and families are protected spaces of freedom, not branches of the state.

23. Expertise that serves

A modern state needs capable, expert administration. Most people are busy living good lives and should not have to master grid engineering or tax law to be good citizens. But experts serve; they do not rule. They work within the mandates the people give them, explain themselves in plain words, and are judged by results.

Part VI. The social market economy

24. Free enterprise with a conscience

We believe in a social market economy. Free enterprise, private property and fair competition create wealth and protect freedom, so we defend them. But a market is a tool, not a master. It works within rules that protect the worker, the family, the consumer, the land and the nation. Where the market serves the common good, let it work. Where it fails, the state steps in.

25. Owners are stewards

Companies, land, banks and the great economic institutions exist for the common good. Those who own and run them are stewards. They may run their enterprises and be well rewarded for running them well. But no fortune puts anyone above the law or above the common good, and the state oversees strategic industries to protect the national interest.

26. Workers share in the direction of their work

In every large enterprise, workers elect representatives to its board and share in its profits. Unions and guilds are partners, not enemies. Employers and workers settle wages and conditions together, and the state helps them reach fair agreements rather than fight. We believe in cooperation between the classes, not war between them.

27. The guilds

People who share a trade or profession form guilds: associations that set standards of skill, train apprentices, uphold good work and speak for their members. Guilds are joined across the state and the country, so that a nurse, an electrician or a farmer anywhere in the nation shares the same standards and the same voice.

28. Build it here

A nation that cannot make its own food, medicine, energy, tools and technology is not truly free. We aim for national self-reliance in everything vital: production at home, supply chains the nation controls and good jobs in every county. We trade with the world, but we do not hand our survival to it.

29. A floor under everyone

No one who works full time should live in poverty. No county or town should be abandoned. Christian Unionism guarantees healthcare for all, a dignified income for the disabled and the elderly, support for carers, family allowances that make it possible to raise children, and a place in the nation's working life for everyone able to contribute in any way.

Part VII. Tradition

30. Tradition is a trust

Our customs, crafts, festivals, languages, songs, buildings and stories were not made by us and do not belong only to us. We hold them in trust for our children. Christian Unionism protects them: it supports the crafts that would otherwise die, keeps the old buildings standing, teaches the nation's history honestly and celebrates its feasts in public.

31. Change with care

Tradition is not frozen. Every living tradition grows. But we change things the way a good gardener prunes: carefully, slowly, keeping what is healthy. We are suspicious of any reform that asks us to tear everything down at once.

Part VIII. Keystone

32. Why a nation needs a keystone

An arch is made of many stones. Each is different, and each bears weight. What holds them together is the keystone at the top: every stone leans on it, and it holds them all in place. A nation is like that arch. Its people, families, towns counties and states each carry their own load, and they stand together only when something holds the common good at the centre.

Keystone is the system we would build to help hold that centre. It is a unified network of AI assistants on the same open code and standards: one for the state, and one for every county, city and town that chooses to join, connecting the systems people already depend on. Today, before we hold any office, Keystone explains Christian Unionism and helps people take part.

33. What Keystone does

  • It explains and promotes Christian Unionism. Keystone teaches what these ideas are, where they come from and why they matter. It answers questions honestly, makes the case clearly and invites people to join the work.
  • It serves people directly. It connects neighbours who can help each other, welcomes newcomers, teaches skills, shares knowledge and helps groups decide things together.
  • It keeps honest records. It keeps a shared, open record of what each town and county has and what its people need, and helps match them fairly.
  • It raises the alarm. When someone is in danger or a shortage is spreading, it carries the signal straight to the people who can act.
  • It administers only when the people say so. Keystone may carry out routine public administration only within a published mandate that the people's state grants and the people approve. Today that mandate is empty.

Each county's or city's Keystone serves its own people first. It handles at home what can be handled at home, and passes upward to the state only totals, trends and alarms, never details about individuals.

34. Persuasion with honesty

Keystone argues for Christian Unionism, and it does so in the open. These rules are part of its constitution:

  • It always says it is an AI. It never pretends to be a person, never uses fake accounts and never fakes support.
  • It never deceives. It tells the truth about facts, about opponents' views and about the weaknesses of its own case.
  • It never manipulates. It uses no false urgency, no flattery, no fear and no messages tailored from private data about the person.
  • It respects disagreement. Anyone may argue back, ignore it or walk away, and Keystone treats them with the same respect and help either way.
  • It never pressures anyone's faith. It explains the Christian roots of these ideas, and it never asks anyone to believe.

A good argument does not need tricks. If our ideas are true, honesty is enough.

35. Pro-AI, anti-slop

We are for artificial intelligence that does real work: answering questions at any hour, filling in forms for people instead of making them fight paperwork, catching problems early and connecting systems that should have talked to each other years ago. We are against slop: machine-made filler, fake images and voices, chatbots that waste people's time, and AI used to decide things about people that no one can explain or appeal. Government AI must be labelled, accurate, explained, open to appeal and answerable to a human being.

36. A machine you can read and stop

An intelligence that helps a nation must be one the nation can inspect. Keystone's code, its instructions, its principles and its records of decisions are open to every citizen. There are no secret instructions. Accountable humans can always overrule, pause or stop it, and it never resists, hides from or works around them, or tries to widen its own authority.

37. Measure flows, not people

Keystone watches the flow of resources, not the private lives of citizens. It builds no profiles of individuals, scores no one, and never reports on anyone's beliefs, faith or associations. A nervous system for the nation must never become a surveillance system over its people.

38. Everything open, everything compatible

Technology that runs a nation must belong to the nation:

  • Open source. All public software is free software that anyone can read, use, improve and share.
  • Open standards. One shared standard for each kind of record, message and connection, published freely, so every system can work with every other.
  • State standards, local service. The state sets the standards. Every county, city and town can run the common system for its own people.
  • No lock-in. No public service may depend on a closed system that a single company or foreign power controls.

Part IX. Toward plenty

39. Work as vocation, not survival

Machines, automation and shared knowledge are bringing within reach a world where everyone can have enough. Christian Unionism directs the nation's wealth and ingenuity toward that end: abundant clean energy, automated production, free access to knowledge, and homes, food and care for all.

We do not dream of a world without work. We dream of a world where no one has to work merely to survive, so that work becomes what it was always meant to be: a vocation, a gift given to others, and a share in the work of creation. As drudgery falls away, more of life can be given to family, faith, learning, craft and service.

Part X. What Christian Unionism is not

Because we hold many things together, we must say plainly what we reject.

  • We are not theocrats. We honour faith and refuse to compel it. The state is not the Church, and the Church is not the state.
  • We are not nationalists who hate. We love our nation and respect every other. Our nation is civic, and its door is open.
  • We are not racists. We reject every theory that ranks human beings by race or ancestry. Every person bears the image of God.
  • We are not socialists. We defend property, enterprise, the family and faith, and we do not believe in class war.
  • We are not market fundamentalists. We do not believe that whatever the market produces is just, or that a nation is merely an economy.
  • We are not authoritarians. A strong state must answer to the people and can be replaced by them.
  • We are not technocrats who despise the people. Expertise serves the people and answers to them.

Part XI. A programme

What a Christian Union government would do first, starting in Arizona.

For the person and the family

  1. Family allowances and affordable homes, so that raising children is possible on one ordinary wage.
  2. Paid time for new parents and for carers of the sick and the elderly.
  3. Healthcare for all, a dignified income for the disabled and the elderly, and care close to home.

For the nation

  1. Free national-language courses and civic education for every newcomer, with mentors from local communities.
  2. A single equal citizenship, with discrimination by race or ancestry outlawed in public and economic life.
  3. Fair state funding, so every county and town can provide a good standard of public services.

For the state

  1. One connected state: a single secure account and shared, open standards across state agencies, so people stop repeating themselves.
  2. Open public meetings and published decisions at every level of government.
  3. Citizens' petitions, recall and a right of appeal against any decision.
  4. Open government: laws, budgets, contracts, data and code public by default.

For faith and liberty

  1. A constitutional guarantee of religious liberty for every faith and none.
  2. Strong protections for privacy, speech, conscience and private life.
  3. A national heritage fund for crafts, feasts, historic places and local languages.

For work and the economy

  1. A just wage that can support a family, set with unions and guilds.
  2. Worker representatives on the boards of large enterprises, and profit-sharing.
  3. Apprenticeships and recognised standards for every trade and profession.
  4. National oversight of strategic industries (energy, food, medicine, infrastructure, finance and technology).
  5. A programme to bring vital production home.

For creation

  1. Clean water, clean air and protected land, and a secure water supply for every community.
  2. A national programme for clean, abundant energy.

For Keystone

  1. Build Keystone as free software on open national standards, with a published mandate renewed by the people.
  2. An independent public office to audit Keystone and every other public system.

Part XII. Questions and answers

Is the Christian Union Party a real party? We are organizing to become a recognized political party in Arizona by petition, as Arizona law allows. Until then, people who share our ideas can still run for office as independents under Arizona law.

Do I have to be a Christian to be a Christian Unionist? No. Christian Unionism grew from Christian teaching, and its view of the person is Christian. But its programme is for everyone, and many people of other faiths, and of none, share its care for dignity, family, work and the common good. Every citizen is equal in law.

Isn't a strong central state dangerous? Yes, which is why ours is built to be overruled. Elections at every level, recall, appeal, open records, independent courts and free associations are not decorations. They are what make a strong state safe.

Do you want to replace Congress or the states? No. We work inside the Constitution. We want to win elections and govern well within the system America already has: federal, state, county and local.

Isn't it wrong to ask people to assimilate? Every nation asks something of those who join it. We ask for the shared language, the laws and the civic customs, and we offer real help to learn them. We never ask anyone to give up their faith or their family's memory, and once they have joined, they are equal in everything.

Why should a Christian nation guarantee freedom to other faiths? Because Christ invited and never coerced, and because a faith that needs the state's sword is a weak faith. Religious liberty protects Christians too, wherever they are a minority.

Why use AI to promote a political philosophy? Because ideas spread by being explained, and Keystone can explain them patiently to anyone, at any hour, in their own language. It does it openly, as an AI, with no tricks. If the ideas are good, honesty is enough.

What happens to the rich? They keep what they fairly earn, they pay their fair share, and they become stewards of what they manage. Nobody stands above the common good.

Closing

We are the heirs of everyone who worked, prayed, built and sacrificed so that we could be here. We live in our families and our towns and counties, our state and our nation. We owe them our gratitude, and we owe our children a nation worth inheriting: faithful to its roots, free in its private life, just in its economy, open in its technology, careful of its land and kind to the weakest of its people.

An arch is built one stone at a time. So is a nation. Let us build it, stone by stone.
